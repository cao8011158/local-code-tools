The journal's recorded mtime is 11:17:48.343 UTC; the main catalog's is 11:18:13.719, the manifest's 11:18:14.939, and publication occurred about 11:18:18. The product build calls `check_catalog_sidecars` before hashing, validating, and publishing. Thus the present sidecar is consistent with a stale sidecar reappearing after that check, possibly through workspace synchronization. The timestamps do not prove which process caused it. Two incidental SQLite-looking dot files (153,092,096 and 241,434,624 bytes) likewise predate publication. Neither their presence nor the valid main catalog authorizes deleting the journal or waiving the product gate.

`recover_verified_checkpoint.py` makes a separately labeled schema-2 **recovery transport**, not a successful product backup from the rejected source. It verifies the manifest/program/config identity, main artifact checksum/size, immutable catalog integrity and node/edge counts, then packages only declared artifacts and required metadata. Production `restore` verifies archive member checksums, compatibility, and staged catalog integrity, rebinds the manifest, and publishes to `/tmp/lct-b3-isolated-20261029`. The observed restore succeeded; the transport SHA-256 is `e00263121819579beb76320aad7d11fc81904c51a0c26da4c0487da183781900` (67,417,243 bytes). The isolated catalog has no sidecar. Product backup should still be executed and verified from the isolated destination before claiming a successful product backup/restore acceptance.

One cache-completeness issue required checking before that claim: the original `vectors.sqlite` has a 12,392-byte WAL and a 32,768-byte SHM, while the initial recovery transport includes only its main file. A frozen copy of main+WAL+SHM at `/tmp/lct-vector-frozen-diagnostic-20261029` was inspected outside the synchronized workspace. Frozen main/WAL/SHM SHA-256 values were `0f9245dfe4c9e595ecf55b2bced13c75892b0248641bb86b67a964907d7502ad`, `191720f1bbbcd444096660698fa618031a19770d9149d25cde9dd5430d6b5331`, and `5f28d4643d71f768edc73c729d138b7563cf0dc91a589bda3def3d114afcfce2`. No vector rows are lost from this lexical-only checkpoint; the manifest also records zero new vectors and requests. This comparison establishes the current logical cache content, not a general guarantee that copying only the main SQLite file is safe for a later dense build. A transactionally consistent SQLite backup from the WAL-aware frozen copy is the stronger recovery transport input.

The supported continuation is to preserve the raw original and its journal, retain the distinct recovery-transport provenance, run product backup and production restore validation against the isolated copy, then perform business graph acceptance there. The catalog evidence supports proceeding with that bounded acceptance. It does not establish full graph import, retrieval, dense index, or remote persistence success.
